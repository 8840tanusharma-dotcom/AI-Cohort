HEALTHCARE CLAIMS PROCESSING, ADJUDICATION, AND APPEALS MANUAL
Standard Operating Guide for Plan Members and Administrators

================================================================================
SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF THE CLAIMS SUBMISSION LIFECYCLE
================================================================================

When you receive medical treatment, healthcare claims follow one of two submission paths depending on whether your service provider participates in our network:

1. In-Network Automatic Filing:
   - Participating network hospitals, clinics, and physicians file claims directly on behalf of the member.
   - The provider accepts the contracted negotiated rate and bills the plan directly.
   - The member is responsible only for applicable deductibles, copayments, or coinsurance stated on the Explanation of Benefits (EOB).

2. Out-of-Network Member-Initiated Filing:
   - If you receive covered emergency care or authorized out-of-network services (Gold PPO), you may need to pay the provider upfront and submit a manual claim form for reimbursement.
   - All manual claims must be submitted to the Claims Department within the timely filing window.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: STEP-BY-STEP CLAIM FILING INSTRUCTIONS
================================================================================

To submit a paper or digital claim for reimbursement, follow these mandatory steps:

Step 1: Obtain the Standard Member Claim Form
- Download Form CLM-2026 (Medical Claim Reimbursement Request) from the member portal.

Step 2: Collect Required Documentation
Every claim packet must include the following verified attachments:
1. Itemized Provider Bill showing:
   - Date(s) of service.
   - Standard Medical Coding: CPT / HCPCS procedure codes and ICD-10 diagnostic codes.
   - Itemized breakdown of individual charges.
   - Rendering provider name, National Provider Identifier (NPI), tax ID, and physical address.
2. Proof of Payment:
   - Credit card receipt, canceled check, or zero-balance receipt showing member payment for out-of-pocket amounts.
3. Medical Necessity Documentation:
   - Operative notes, diagnostic imaging reports (e.g., X-ray, MRI reports), or doctor referral letters when required.

Step 3: Submit the Completed Packet
- Online Submission: Upload PDF scans through the Secure Member Web Portal.
- Mail Submission: Send original claim forms and copies of itemized receipts to:
  Claims Processing Center, Attention: Member Services, PO Box 4020, Healthcare Operations.

Step 4: Submission Deadlines (Timely Filing Limit)
- Standard Claims: Must be submitted within 90 calendar days from the date the medical service was rendered.
- Foreign / Out-of-Country Emergency Claims: Must be submitted within 180 calendar days from the date of service, accompanied by certified English translations and currency conversion documentation.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: CLAIM ADJUDICATION STATUSES AND DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

Once received, claims are processed through the automated adjudication engine. Member claims display one of the following statuses:

1. Pending / Under Review:
   - The claim has been logged and is awaiting clinical review, fee verification, or supplemental documentation from the provider or member. Average processing duration: 10 to 14 business days.

2. Approved:
   - The service is verified as covered under the plan schedule. Payment has been calculated according to deductible and coinsurance rules, and payment has been authorized to the provider or member.

3. Partially Approved / Adjusted:
   - A portion of the claim has been approved, while certain line items were disallowed (e.g., non-covered supplies, charge exceeding maximum allowable rate).

4. Denied:
   - The claim has been rejected. Common denial reason codes include:
     * ERR-01: Service not covered under member benefit schedule.
     * ERR-02: Missing required Prior Authorization before procedure date.
     * ERR-03: Timely filing limit exceeded (submitted after 90-day deadline).
     * ERR-04: Out-of-network provider utilized without valid PCP referral (HMO plans).
     * ERR-05: Duplicate claim submission.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: READING YOUR EXPLANATION OF BENEFITS (EOB)
================================================================================

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is generated for every adjudicated claim. It is not a bill. The EOB contains:
- Billed Amount: The gross amount submitted by the medical provider.
- Plan Allowed Amount: The negotiated contracted maximum rate permitted for the procedure.
- Plan Paid: The amount paid by your healthcare policy.
- Member Responsibility: The balance owed by you to the provider, representing your copay, unmet annual deductible, or coinsurance percentage.

================================================================================
SECTION 5: DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND FORMAL APPEALS PROCESS
================================================================================

If your claim is partially or fully denied, you possess the legal right to appeal the determination:

Level 1: Internal Administrative Appeal
- Filing Window: You must submit a written Appeal Request Form within 180 calendar days of receiving your denial notice (EOB).
- Supporting Evidence: Include medical records, physician letters of medical necessity, and clinical rationale.
- Decision Timeline: Standard appeals are resolved within 30 calendar days for post-service claims (or 72 hours for urgent/pre-service life-threatening conditions).

Level 2: External Independent Review
- If the Level 1 internal appeal upholds the denial based on medical judgment or experimental exclusion, you may request an Independent External Review within 4 months of the final internal adverse decision.
- An accredited independent physician panel will review the case. Their ruling is legally binding on the insurance plan.
